# Pre-Integration Data Quality Assessment

## 1. Hospital General Information Dataset

Completeness: The Hospital General Information dataset contains 5,419 records. All nine hospital identification and descriptive variables assessed, including Facility ID, Facility Name, address, city/town, state, ZIP code, county/parish, hospital type, and hospital ownership, have no missing values. However, Hospital Overall rating is missing for 2,245 records (41.4%). Although this limits the usefulness of overall hospital ratings, the completeness of Facility ID supports integration with the other CMS datasets.

Accuracy: The Hospital General Information dataset was assessed for accuracy by examining Hospital Overall Rating values and Facility ID formatting. All Hospital Overall Rating values fall within the expected range of 1-5 or are marked as "Not Available," indicating that no invalid rating values were identified. Additionally, all Facility IDs follow the expected six-character format, with leading zeros preserved where applicable.

Since the dataset is published by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), it is considered a credible source of hospital information. However, the accuracy of individual hospital names, addresses, and reported rating was not independently verified against external sources. Therefore, while the assessed values appear valid, their accuracy cannot be fully confirmed without additional verification. 

Timeliness: The Hospital General Information dataset was last modified on October 26, 2026, so it is considered timely for the purposes of this project. However, this date reflects the dataset's modification rather than necessarily the reporting periods of the underlying hospital ratings. Additional examination of the reporting periods in the HCAHPS and Complications and Deaths datasets will be necessary to determine whether the datasets are compatible for integration.

Consistency: The Hospital General Information dataset was assessed for consistency by examining Facility IDs, state abbreviations, and other hospital identification variables. All Facility IDs follow a consistent six-character format, and no duplicate Facility IDs were identified among the 5,419 records. State values are consistently represented using two-letter abbreviations, and Hospital Overall Ratings follow a standardized scale of 1-5 or are marked as "Not Available."

Overall, no significant formatting inconsistencies or identification issues were found in the variables assessed. The hospital identification variables are complete and consistently formatted, suggesting that hospitals can be reliably identified within the dataset. Additionally, the uniqueness and consistent formatting of Facility IDs support their use as the primary key when integrating the three CMS datasets.

## 2. HCAHPS Patient Survey Dataset

The full HCAHPS Patient Survey file contains 512,209 records and 22 variables covering 4,787 hospitals. Most of these rows are answer-level percentages (for example, the percent of patients who answered "Always"). Our research questions only need one summary score per hospital per measure, so we reduced the file to the 52,657 "linear mean score" rows and 11 columns using scripts/preprocessing/reduce_hcahps_data.py. The assessment below covers the reduced file.

Completeness: Every hospital has exactly 11 linear-score measures (4,787 hospitals × 11 measures = 52,657 rows), and the identification variables (Facility ID, Facility Name, State, Measure ID, Question, Start Date, End Date) have no missing values. However, 17,666 of the 52,657 linear mean values (33.5%) are "Not Available." Missingness is all-or-nothing at the hospital level: 1,606 hospitals have no scores for any measure, while the remaining 3,181 hospitals have all 11 scores. No hospital has partial scores. Number of Completed Surveys is "Not Available" for 847 hospitals. Every missing score has a CMS footnote code in the Number of Completed Surveys Footnote column (codes 1, 5, 6, 10 and 19 are the most common), so missing values are explained by CMS rather than being unexplained gaps.

Accuracy: Since the dataset is published by CMS, it is considered a credible source of patient experience information. All available linear mean values are whole numbers between 58 and 100, which falls within the documented 0-100 linear scale. Medians range from 77 (communication about medicines) to 91 (nurse and doctor communication). Number of Completed Surveys ranges from 25 to 11,368 per hospital (median 425). Small survey counts make some scores less reliable, so we kept this column for the analysis. Individual hospital results were not independently verified against outside sources.

Timeliness: All records share a single reporting period, January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2025, which makes this the most recent of our three datasets. The Complications and Deaths measures use earlier, multi-year windows ending between March and June 2025, so the HCAHPS period overlaps them only for January to June 2025. This difference in reporting periods is a limitation that will be noted in the analysis. Also, CMS updated the HCAHPS survey for 2025: the Quietness measure from earlier releases was replaced by Restfulness of Environment, and Care Coordination, Staff Responsiveness, and Symptoms After Stay were added. Nurse communication and doctor communication, which our research questions focus on, are unchanged.

Consistency: All Facility IDs are six-character strings. Some end in "F" (164 hospitals, for example 01014F), so IDs must be kept as text rather than numbers. There are no duplicate Facility ID and Measure ID pairs, and no leading or trailing whitespace in any column. Each Measure ID always maps to the same question text, dates use one MM/DD/YYYY format, and missing values are always written as "Not Available." Patient Survey Star Rating is "Not Applicable" on every linear-score row, so it carries no information for this subset.

Integration feasibility: All 4,787 HCAHPS Facility IDs appear in the Complications and Deaths dataset, and 4,768 appear in Hospital General Information. The 19 hospitals missing from Hospital General Information have no HCAHPS scores, so no usable patient experience data is lost. In total, 3,149 hospitals have HCAHPS scores and also appear in both other datasets. Facility names differ from Hospital General Information for 42 hospitals (mostly renamed hospitals, such as WATERBURY HOSPITAL vs. UCONN HEALTH WATERBURY HOSPITAL), which confirms Facility ID rather than name should be the join key. Because the file is in long format (one row per hospital per measure), it will be pivoted to one row per hospital before integration.

## 3. Complications and Deaths Dataset

Completeness: The Hospital Complications and Deaths dataset contains 95,740 records and 18 variables. These include hospital identification variables such as Facility ID, Facility Name, Address, City/Town, State, ZIP Code, County/Parish, and Telephone Number, as well as measure-specific variables such as Measure ID, Measure Name, Compared to National, Denominator, Score, Lower Estimate, Higher Estimate, Footnote, Start Date, and End Date. The dataset contains multiple records for individual hospitals because each hospital reports results for multiple quality measures and reporting periods. Therefore, repeated Facility IDs are expected and do not necessarily indicate duplicate records. Approximately 46% of Score entries are missing, which limits the ability to evaluate certain hospital outcomes. However, the remaining variables are complete, allowing hospitals to be identified and other measure-specific information to be analyzed.

Accuracy: Since the dataset is published by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), it is considered a credible source of hospital quality information. However, individual hospital results were not independently verified against external sources. Score values are heavily concentrated between 1 and 20, although many extend up to 100 and one reaches 110. While this range may initially appear unusual, the available documentation does not clearly establish the acceptable range for every measure. 

Timeliness: The dataset includes reporting periods with ending dates as recent as June 30, 2025. While this provides relatively recent historical information, the absence of reporting periods after that date suggests that newer hospital performance data may not be reflected. The dataset remains useful for analyzing hospital outcomes, although it may not represent the most current performance.

Consistency: The dataset was assessed for consistency by examining Facility IDs, state abbreviations, and other hospital identification variables. Facility IDs follow a consistent six-character format, while state values use two-letter abbreviations. Formatting for other variables, including dates, remained consistent as well. The only inconsistency was that the comparison to national benchmarks contained some values that varied slightly but ultimately conveyed the same message. 

Overall, there were no major formatting or consistency issues identified that couldn't be easily addressed. The hospitals in the dataset can be easily identified, and although many entries did not contain a score, the vast majority do allow for analysis to be done. The uniqueness and formatting of Facility IDs allows for identification across all three datasets which ultimately preserves the usefulness and ease of use of the dataset.
